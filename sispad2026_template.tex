\begin{abstract}
We investigate vibrationally assisted transport in a ZnO/Mg\textsubscript{0.3}Zn\textsubscript{0.7}O resonant tunneling diode using a one-dimensional Non-Equilibrium Green's Function calculation with local self-consistent Born scattering. Synthetic molecular modes at 100 and 180 meV modify the calculated current at 300 K. The archived calculations are per transverse mode and per spin, use a prescribed electrostatic potential, and do not implement the proposed multimode rank-1 extension. Numerical revalidation finds substantial energy-grid sensitivity in the molecular second derivative, so the archived spectra do not yet establish quantitative room-temperature discrimination or whole-device sensitivity. We identify the required checks on spectral refinement, transverse coupling, and self-consistent electrostatics before those predictions can be made.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Inelastic Electron Tunneling Spectroscopy (IETS) provides vibrational fingerprints of molecules adsorbed in or near a tunnel junction by introducing weak inelastic openings of additional transport channels at molecule-specific biases, observable as peaks in $d^2I/dV^2$~\cite{hansma1977,hansma1982,hipps1993,reed2008}. It has been widely used for studying electron-phonon inelastic interaction and vibrational spectroscopy of molecules and organic and inorganic thin films~\cite{jeong2015,liu2010,kim2011,wang2004,lauhon2001}. 

In 1996, Turin proposed that biological olfaction itself may be an IETS-like process, with olfactory receptors sensing the vibrational energies of odorant molecules rather than (or in addition to) their shape~\cite{turin1996}. This proposition has motivated technological interest in a \emph{biomimetic} electronic nose that identifies odorants by their intrinsic vibrational signature~\cite{patil2018,bommisetty2008,patil2013mit, li2024}. Recent computational work from this group has shown that unsupervised clustering on the vibrational spectra of 20 odorants recovers their perceptual odor categories and even resolves subclasses~\cite{pandey2021}. Existing electronic-nose technologies span resistive, capacitive, gravimetric and optical transduction \cite{rock2008}; but mimicking natural olfaction remains an open challenge with applications in security, environmental monitoring, healthcare, and food and agriculture. Its attainable sensitivity and chemical coverage require separate validation.

The obstacle to a deployable IETS-based e-nose is resolution at non-cryogenic temperatures. The Lambe--Jaklevic analysis of thermal Fermi-distribution softening gives a best-case spectral resolution of $5.4 k_B T$, which at 300~K is $\approx 140$~meV, comparable to odorant vibrational modes. IETS was for decades considered unusable above $\sim$150~K~\cite{jaklevic1966}. Two recent developments challenge this. Ngabonziza~\textit{et al.}~\cite{ngabonziza2021} reported the first inelastic-tunneling spectra above room temperature, achieving 20~meV resolution at 400~K in BaZrO$_3$-based proton-conducting defect-mediated tunnel junctions and demonstrating that the $5.4 k_B T$ bound is not fundamental but a property of conventional MIM geometries. Earlier work from this group~\cite{patil2018} showed that a double-barrier resonant tunneling diode (RTD) achieves the same: its narrow transmission resonance acts as an intrinsic energy filter whose effective width is set by the resonance linewidth $\Gamma_{\mathrm{res}}$, not $k_BT$, preserving $d^2I/dV^2$ features at 300~K in a toy device. 

We extend this concept to a manufacturable all-oxide stack: ZnO/Mg$_{0.3}$Zn$_{0.7}$O, which to our knowledge is the only published BEOL-compatible oxide pair with conduction-band offset in the $\Delta E_c \approx 0.5$~eV window and experimentally demonstrated NDR \cite{krishnamoorthy2002}. We examine molecular contributions in a single-transverse-mode NEGF--SCBA model; quantitative spectral discrimination remains subject to numerical and model validation. In a sensor system, each $10\,\mu$m $\times$ $10\,\mu$m RTD would be one device in an array, with the $d^2I/dV^2$ spectrum measured using standard AC lock-in techniques or DC measurement with noise filtering~\cite{kesarwani2022}

%For a Gaussian molecular approximation ($\sigma_{\mathrm{mol}} \approx 3$~\AA), the electron-vibron coupling factorizes as $M = D_0\, \chi(z)\, |u\rangle\langle u|$ in the transverse-mode basis (correction $O(10^{-6})$). This reduces the 3D SCBA to a scalar 1D projected Dyson equation, exact in the continuum limit.

\section{Device and Method}
\textbf{Device.} The symmetric RTD consists of 2~nm Mg$_{0.3}$Zn$_{0.7}$O barriers, a 3~nm ZnO well, and 10~nm $n^+$-ZnO contacts ($N_D = 5\times10^{24}$~m$^{-3}$). Material parameters used in the simulations are $\Delta E_c = 0.47$~eV, $m^* = 0.28\, m_0$, and a bulk ZnO longitudinal-optical (LO) phonon energy $\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{LO}} \approx 72$~meV. The archived single-mode calculation has NDR near $V_{\mathrm{res}} \approx 560$~mV with peak current $\sim$69~nA \emph{per transverse mode per spin} (Fig.~\ref{fig:device}). A proposed $10\,\mu$m $\times$ $10\,\mu$m footprint does not enter this current; obtaining its total current requires a transverse transport model. Candidate material stacks for room-temperature IETS are compared in Table~\ref{tab:stacks}; ZnO/Mg$_{0.3}$Zn$_{0.7}$O uniquely satisfies all three criteria (CBO in vibrational range, demonstrated NDR, BEOL deposition $<400^\circ$C).

\textbf{NEGF transport.} Longitudinal discretization uses a $0.2$~nm tight-binding grid with effective-mass kinetic terms. The retarded Green's function $G^R(E) = [(E + i\eta)\mathbb{1} - H - \Sigma^L - \Sigma^R - \Sigma^{\mathrm{ph}}]^{-1}$ is in the standard form with semi-infinite-lead self-energies \cite{datta_textbook}, symmetric bias ($\mu_{L,R} = E_F \pm V/2$, $E_F = 20$~meV above the ZnO conduction-band edge, the degenerate value fixed by charge neutrality at the quoted contact doping), and a causal phonon self-energy $\Sigma^{\mathrm{ph}}$ solved within the self-consistent Born approximation (SCBA). 

\textbf{Implemented model and proposed extension.} The production calculation solves one transverse channel with local, longitudinally diagonal phonon self-energies. The molecular coupling strength has a Gaussian longitudinal envelope $\chi(z)$ at the emitter barrier. A separate algebraic module explores the approximation $M(z)\approx D_0\chi(z)|u\rangle\langle u|$ for a transversely localized molecule, but the multimode Keldysh reconstruction and its self-consistent transport are not implemented in the reported pipeline. Consequently these data do not demonstrate a reduction of the full three-dimensional scattering problem. Integrating independent one-dimensional channels also omits molecular intermode coupling.

\textbf{Bulk + molecular phonons.} The bulk ZnO LO phonon ($\hbar\omega_{\mathrm{LO}} = 72$~meV) couples uniformly in the transverse plane ($D_{\mathrm{bulk}}^2 = 0.001$~eV$^2$/site, $\chi \equiv 1$ in the active region). Molecular vibrons are added on top with $D_0^2 = 0.1$~eV$^2$ and a Gaussian $\chi(z)$ centered on the emitter barrier. 

\textbf{Self-consistent electrostatics.} The archived figures use a fixed linear potential and omit space-charge feedback. Earlier supplementary Poisson estimates of the peak shift were unconverged and are withdrawn; neither a rigid shift of the spectrum nor preservation of its internal structure has been established. Current exploratory Poisson calculations use a windowed single-mode scattering-to-ballistic density ratio applied to transverse-integrated ballistic charge. This is an approximate charge closure, not a full transverse-integrated scattering calculation. Its validation requires converged inner solves, an unscreened Poisson equation residual, and grid and model sensitivity checks~\cite{lake1997}.

\textbf{Numerical validation.} The archived 2~meV energy grid and 16~mV bias grid reproduce the saved currents, but reproduction is not a discretization test. The maximum archived relative contact-current imbalance is 0.65\% for Baseline and 1.98\% for Mol\_AB; the earlier blanket claim of less than 0.2\% is withdrawn. With the SCBA tolerance tightened to $10^{-7}$, changing the energy spacing from 2 to 0.1~meV changes the Mol\_A-minus-Baseline second derivative at 560~mV from 1.422 to 0.625~$\mu$A/V$^2$ on the same 16~mV voltage stencil. Thus the molecular spectrum is not grid-converged. New calculations must test both energy and bias refinement, in addition to solver convergence and current conservation.

\textbf{Analytes.} Three synthetic molecules are studied: Mol\_A ($\hbar\omega_A = 100$~meV), Mol\_B ($\hbar\omega_B = 180$~meV), Mol\_AB (both modes), and a Baseline with only the bulk 72~meV phonon. These mode energies lie in the vibrational-fingerprint band (50--200~meV) characteristic of small organic odorants~\cite{pandey2021}.

\section{Results and Discussion}

\subsection{I--V and NDR characterization}
All analytes show clean NDR near $V_{\mathrm{res}} \approx 560$~mV (Fig.~\ref{fig:iv}). The archived Baseline peak current is $\sim$69~nA per transverse mode per spin with a peak-to-valley ratio (PVR) $\approx 12$, consistent with the values expected from a 2/3/2~nm ZnO/Mg$_{0.3}$Zn$_{0.7}$O stack at 300~K. The molecular vibrons reduce the elastic transmission by opening additional inelastic channels, leading to molecule-dependent step features in $I(V)$ on top of the elastic NDR background.

\subsection{Molecular contribution and its limitations}
The diagnostic $\Delta D(V)=d^2I_{\mathrm{mol}}/dV^2-d^2I_{\mathrm{BL}}/dV^2$ isolates differences between molecular and Baseline calculations. Phonon-assisted channels can generate such differences, but their voltage positions depend on resonance alignment and electrostatic lever arms; a generic identification $V=\hbar\omega/e$ is not justified here. The archived Mol\_AB correction is approximately additive, an empirical observation for these parameters, not a consequence of orthogonal rank-1 projections. Because the derivatives change substantially on energy-grid refinement, the archived deviations alone do not establish resolved molecular fingerprints.

\subsection{Temperature sweep and sensor implications}
The archived temperature sweep holds $E_F=20$~meV fixed. In the parabolic-band model this corresponds to approximately $5.04\times10^{24}$~m$^{-3}$ at 300~K and $1.91\times10^{24}$~m$^{-3}$ at 10~K, so it changes carrier density as well as temperature. It does not establish temperature robustness at fixed doping. Revised sweeps must solve contact neutrality for $E_F(T)$ at fixed ionized donor density and converge the molecular derivatives. Experimental sensitivity, noise, and a whole-pixel response have not been calculated.

\section{Conclusion}
The implemented one-dimensional local NEGF--SCBA model produces molecular changes to transport in a ZnO/MgZnO RTD. It remains a proof-of-concept calculation: the molecular second derivative is not numerically converged, the reported current is per mode and per spin, and the proposed multimode scattering extension is not implemented. Quantitative room-temperature discrimination requires spectral refinement, fixed-doping temperature sweeps, and validation of transverse scattering and electrostatic charge before comparison with experiment.
